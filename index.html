Cyberbullying
isn't just online.

To what extent does cyberbullying influence the mental health of high school students across different cultural and social contexts?

This interactive resource explores the issue through evidence, different perspectives, possible solutions and a final conclusion.

01

One global issue

Cyberbullying can happen through social media, messages, games and websites. It can reach many people and happen at any time.

01 • The issue

What is cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is bullying that happens online. It can include insults, threats, spreading rumors or sharing private information.

01

Online spaces

It can occur on social media, messaging services, games or websites.

02

Always connected

Because teenagers use the internet every day, cyberbullying can happen at any time and reach many people.

03

A global issue

Different countries have different laws and ways of dealing with cyberbullying, while social and cultural differences can affect how students react and ask for help.

02 • Evidence

How can it affect mental health?

The presentation identifies several possible mental-health and school-related effects.

Anxiety

Cyberbullying can be associated with increased anxiety among adolescents.

Depression

Research cited in the presentation links cyberbullying with depression and emotional distress.

Stress

Students may experience increased levels of stress and emotional pressure.

Loneliness

Cyberbullying can contribute to feelings of isolation and loneliness.

Low self-esteem

The presentation identifies low self-esteem as another possible effect.

School problems

The impact can also affect students' experiences at school.

Note: The source presentation also mentions serious cases involving self-harm or suicidal thoughts. This website does not describe those experiences in detail.

05 • Sources & evaluation

What does the research say?

The presentation uses research-based sources and evaluates both their strengths and limitations.

National Institutes of Health

Research cited in the presentation says cyberbullying has serious effects on adolescents' mental health, with victims showing higher levels of anxiety, depression and emotional distress.

RSIS International

The presentation highlights that cyberbullying affects students in different countries and cultures, and that family support, schools and awareness can reduce its impact.

Source limitations: Some studies focus on specific countries, and online behavior changes quickly, so research needs constant updating.

06 • Possible solutions

What can we do?

The proposed responses focus on prevention, support, education and safer online environments.

1

Digital citizenship

Teach students how to behave responsibly and safely online.

2

Reporting

Encourage students to report cyberbullying instead of ignoring it.

3

School support

Increase mental health support available in schools.

4

Family support

Parents and families can pay attention to online activity and provide support.

5

Platform safety

Social media platforms should improve their safety tools.

07 • Conclusion

What can be learned?

Cyberbullying has a significant impact on teenagers' mental health worldwide. Although cultures and social contexts are different, the emotional consequences can be similar.

Education, prevention, family support and school programs are essential to reduce its effects. The strongest response is therefore not just punishment or persuasion: it is a combination of prevention, support and safer digital environments.
